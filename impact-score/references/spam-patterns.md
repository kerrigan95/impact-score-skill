# Link spam patterns

Check every publisher against these. When one matches, name it in the report and show the evidence. Based on Bazoom's guide "Link Spam in iGaming and Crypto: Where It Comes From and How to Spot It" (bazoom.com), but the patterns apply in every niche.

## The nine patterns

| Pattern | Where it's sold | How it works | How to spot it |
|---|---|---|---|
| PBN posts | Guest post lists, Telegram sellers, "high DR" bundles | Expired domains rebuilt as blogs that exist to sell links | Wayback shows a different business. Outbound links to unrelated money sites. Same theme or host as other sellers' sites |
| Inflated "premium" sites | Marketplaces, outreach emails with price sheets | DR pumped with cheap links, traffic pumped with bots or one lucky page | Traffic doesn't match ranking keywords. DR climbs while traffic stays flat |
| Parasite sections | Resellers of "news site" placements | A casino or crypto subfolder rented on a trusted news domain | A /casino/ or /crypto/ folder unrelated to the rest of the site. Google's site reputation abuse policy targets this, so the link can vanish overnight |
| Hacked-site injections | Black-hat sellers, cheap "edu/gov link" offers | Links hidden in old, unpatched sites | Anchor or page language doesn't match the site. Links in old posts with no editorial reason |
| Bulk niche edits | "Aged domain" insertion packages | Links dropped into old, unrelated articles in bulk | Link first seen years after the article was published. Many commercial links added to one page |
| Automated packages | "500 backlinks for $49" gigs, tiered tools | Software posts profiles, comments and wiki links at scale | Thousands of referring domains with UR near 0. Profiles, comments and nofollow dominate |
| Expired domain redirects | Domain auctions, "authority transfer" services | An old domain's links are 301-redirected to you | Links show as redirects from domains in a different niche |
| Negative SEO attacks | Competitors, or spam sellers using a site as a billboard | Thousands of toxic links in days | Referring domains go vertical with no campaign. Telegram handles, pharma or adult anchors |
| Paid AI-bait listicles | "AI visibility" or "LLM citation" packages | Brand seeded into near-identical "best X" lists across many sites | The same list, same order, on several sites in the same week. No sponsored label |

## The 5-minute publisher check

Fail two of these and walk away.

| Check | How to read it | Healthy | Red flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Traffic per keyword | Organic traffic ÷ organic keywords | Single digits to low tens | Hundreds or thousands |
| DR vs traffic over 12 months | DR history next to the traffic graph | Move together, or DR lags traffic | DR climbs while traffic is flat or falling |
| Referring domain velocity | New referring domains per month | Steady, uneven growth | One month several times the normal, with no news or campaign behind it |
| Outbound linking | Linked domains vs referring domains, then outgoing anchors | Links to sources and partners in its topic | Links out to far more domains than link in, with casino, CBD or loan anchors |
| Topic spread | Top pages by traffic | 1 to 3 related topics | Cars on Monday, CBD on Tuesday, mortgages on Wednesday |
| Traffic geography | Organic traffic by country | Matches the site's language and market | A Danish site with most traffic from countries it doesn't write for |
| Its own anchor cloud | Anchors report | Brand name, URLs, natural phrases | Money keywords, foreign-language spam, Telegram handles |

Two checks outside SEO tools are worth 30 seconds each: look at the domain in the Wayback Machine to see what it used to be, and read one recent article. If no editor would sign off on it, nobody is protecting the page your link lives on.

## Anchor cloud and keyword checks

Run these on every prospect. Show the actual anchors or keywords as evidence.

**How to measure the dofollow share.** List the top anchors with their link and dofollow counts, mark which rows are spam, then add up dofollow links ÷ all links for exactly those rows. Use the real Ahrefs numbers for every row you marked. Never estimate, and never assume dofollow when the counts are missing: unconfirmed spam is a watch note, not a deduction. The big spam anchors are usually long sentences with thousands of nofollow links, so leaving one out can swing the share completely.

**Not outbound spam:** links to other legitimate topics (health, lifestyle, local news, sister publications in the same media group, sources, sponsors), even when they're off-topic for the site. Outbound spam means the spam themes above or foreign-script spam.

**Dofollow decides.** Spam anchors only count against a site when the links are mostly dofollow, because only then do they pass value and affect the site. Spam that's mostly nofollow (very common with negative SEO and comment spam) never lowers the score. It becomes a "Spam to watch" note: the site is fine to use today, but check it again in a few months. Off-topic keyword traffic only counts when it's the majority of the site's traffic.

| Check | Data | Red flag | If mostly dofollow / majority | If mostly nofollow / minority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adult or pharma anchors in | Top inbound anchors with dofollow counts | Porn, escort, adult, viagra, pills or pharma anchors | Major, −2 | Watch note |
| Injected spam anchors in | Top inbound anchors with dofollow counts | Casino, slots, lottery (for example Vietnamese xổ số, XSMN, KQXSMN), IPTV, loans, crypto pumps, "SEO backlinks", PBN offers, Telegram handles, reputation-laundering names stuffed as anchors, or foreign-script anchors in a language the site doesn't publish in | Heavy: major, −2. Light: −0.75 | Watch note |
| Random anchors | Top inbound anchors with dofollow counts | Three or more anchors like 88AA, OPEN88, KQXSMN, comment-46123, 1234567 | −1 | Watch note |
| Spam anchors out | Top outbound anchors with dofollow counts | The site links out with spam-theme or foreign-script anchors unrelated to its topic. Usually hacked pages or spam user profiles (look for /author/, /members/ or /community/ paths), or link selling | Major, −2 | Watch note |
| Off-topic keyword traffic | Top keywords by traffic | Traffic from keywords unrelated to the site's topic: booster, parasite or slop traffic | Over 50%: major, −1.5 | 25 to 50%: watch note |
| Own-name traffic | Top keywords by traffic | Over 60% of traffic from searches for the site's own name, with 1,000+ monthly visits, on a site that isn't a well-known brand | Major, −1.5 | |

**Strong-site protection.** If relevance and metric quality (Domain trust before any spam deductions) are both 7 or higher and there are no major red flags, the verdict is at least Negotiate, with any spam shown as a watch note.

Context matters:
- Generic anchors (here, website, click here), URLs, the brand name and article titles are normal.
- On a site whose own topic is gambling or crypto, gambling and crypto anchors are on-topic.
- Names of the site's subject are on-topic keywords, not branded ones. "fc barcelona" on a Barcelona fan site is the topic, not the site's brand.
- Inbound spam is often a negative SEO attack the site didn't ask for, and most of it is nofollow. Heavy dofollow inbound spam plus dofollow spam outbound anchors means the site itself is compromised: that's two major flags and an automatic Skip.

## Not spam on their own

People often reject good sites for these. Treat them as notes, not red flags:
- A "Sponsored" or "Partner" label. It's disclosure. Negotiate price or placement if it matters to you.
- A moderate traffic dip that matches a known core update across the whole niche.
- A modest DR on a tightly topical site with real traffic.
- A "Write for us" page on an otherwise editorial site. Check the articles, not the page.
- A high outbound link ratio on a news site or directory. They link out by nature; only above 5x is it a red flag.
- Press release or newswire sections on a real publication.
- A target in a regulated niche (gambling, high-risk payments, crypto). That's the client's industry, not a spam signal.
